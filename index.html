How to read this, and what it is not

Each coloured cell is the LHASA XGBoost probability that a rainfall-triggered landslide occurs in that ~1 km cell on a day with the slider conditions. It is a model output, not a measured frequency for that hillside, and not a flood forecast. The model saturates near 1 under extreme sliders — that is what the model says, not a claim that a landslide is certain.

Inputs, in model order: Lithology, Slope, PGA, soil moisture (gwetprof), antecedent rain, rain ÷ p99. Static layers are the 30-arcsecond (~1 km) LHASA static files, clipped to 97–106°E, 5.5–20.5°N (1080 × 1800 cells). Dynamic inputs here are scenario sliders, not live IMERG/SMAP data — a live version would still need a daily feed for those three.

Tap or click any cell to see its real terrain values, p99, rain ratio and probability — the tapped cell is outlined in red with a yellow fill, so you can see exactly which 1 km cell the numbers belong to. Or type a latitude/longitude in the “Go to cell” box above the map (or edit the lat/lon in the cell panel) and it zooms to that cell, outlines it the same way, and shows its values. Drag to pan, pinch or use the +/− control to zoom. On a phone, one finger scrolls the page and two fingers move the map, and the “↓ Sliders below” button over the map jumps to the sliders.

Exposure is shown only for the tapped cell, in the panel above: that cell's population and road length from NASA's exposure files, and how much of each counts toward exposure at NASA's thresholds (low p > 0.1, medium p > 0.5, high p > 0.9). The thresholds are nested — a high cell also counts at low and medium — and there is no province or country total on this site.

Alignment: the data grid is equirectangular but the basemap is Web Mercator, so the overlay is warped row-by-row to Mercator latitudes before drawing — otherwise it drifts by up to ~80 px at zoom 10 and ~315 px at zoom 12. The probability and grid canvases are rendered as MapLibre CanvasSource raster layers inside the map (below the basemap's labels, so street names sit on top), not as DOM overlays — a DOM overlay lags the map's own transform during a pan or pinch, which made the layer look like it was resizing while moving. Colours are a gradient sampled from NASA's Global Landslide Susceptibility legend (Very Low #0f5fc2 at 0.10, Low #6a9cd9 at 0.30, Medium #c2c262 at 0.50, High #c2860e at 0.70, Very High #c21010 at 0.90, darker red at 1.00) — the colours are NASA's, the probability anchors are ours, since v2 does not publish a 5-level probability scale. The layer sits at 60% opacity by default; the opacity slider adjusts it.

Basemap: OpenFreeMap vector positron, © OpenStreetMap contributors, © OpenMapTiles. Model: NASA LHASA v2.1.1, XGBoost, 300 depth-2 trees, binary logistic, base score 0.1246 (used as logit). Converted to a 45 KB stripped JSON; traversal verified against the real XGBoost Booster in Python (max difference < 4×10−7). Static data: LHASA static.zip (Slope, Lithology, PGA, mask, p99), 2024-08-07 package. Thailand outline for clipping: GADM v4.1 level 0, simplified to 0.01°. Sources: github.com/nasa/LHASA.
